Volume-complete distance vs. sensitivity horizon

V = N / ρ; r = [3V / (4π fsky)]⅓. The volume-complete distance is a count-equivalent estimate, not a demonstrated complete sphere. The J-or-W2 horizon uses max(dJ, dW2) at each type, then averages volume within each published bin.

References, cache & interpretation

Best et al. (2024), Table 3 gives densities for [L0,L1), …, [Y1,Y2), and Y2 itself. Close binaries count as one unresolved source in that reference; separately cataloged resolved components can therefore bias this comparison. Y bins have sparse calibrators, especially Y2. No density is extrapolated to M dwarfs or later Y types.

MOCAdb field-dwarf J (mixed J systems) and AllWISE W2 sequences are interpolated within their stored validity ranges. Their systematic photometric-system differences are not corrected. W2 scatter is missing in the current relation, so the envelope omits that uncertainty. The default empirical depth comes from current SPHEREx synthetic photometry errors, deduplicated per object and band, without quality or S/N cuts. It is conditional on objects with stored positive-flux magnitudes and valid errors: not a uniform sky-depth map, and potentially biased by source noise and source selection. The displayed 16–84% depth spread is not an error on the median.

A count deficit can reflect footprint, depth variations, missing/untyped catalog entries, contamination, multiplicity, extinction or selection. Catalog sources lacking a preferred type are absent. A definitive radial completeness or missing-late-T result requires a sky selection function and distance-dependent recovery test. The uniform local density approximation can also break down at large distances. Edit depths and sky fraction to assess these effects. The four-survey mission forecast is not substituted for the current data release.

Data definitions, missing values & provenance

The default type priority is the private-adopted literature/visual (calculation_method IS NULL), nonphotometric type, then the public-adopted literature/visual (calculation_method IS NULL), nonphotometric type, then a stored SPHEREx autotype ranked by spectral review. The review tiers are good / peculiar_ucd / late_M, then contaminated_ucd / good_plus_star, then good_candidate, then incomplete_but_promising, then other or unreviewed. Within a tier, SPIFFStacker has priority over SPIFF and SUBLIMEaperture. Each lane uses its newest non-ignored stored type. The spectrum panels refit the newest active spectrum in each reduction, or the newest ignored spectrum when that is all that is available. Ignored spectra are labeled. A newly computed best fit can differ from the stored type used for sorting.

Known UCDs have first priority for plot colors, followed by Known UCD candidates (public_adopted=1, photometric_estimate=1). BYW membership, a BYW, Yadu or Azul claim, or an SMDET designation then puts an object in Claimed UCDs regardless of its motion or spectral review. For the motion categories, at least one review in SPIFFStacker, SPIFF or SUBLIMEaperture must be good, good_candidate, contaminated_ucd, peculiar_ucd or good_plus_star. With that spectral quality, new motion-confirmed means motion ≥ 5; new candidate means motion ≥ 3 and < 5; still candidates means motion from 1 through 2 inclusive. All remaining selected objects are Others. These mutually exclusive classes categorize the objects passing the current filters. Star-shaped scatter markers pass the selected spectral confirmation standard and have motion score ≥ 5, including within Claimed UCDs and Known recovered. The dropdown chooses whether good_candidate counts; good includes contaminated_ucd, peculiar_ucd and good_plus_star. Other objects retain regular marker shapes. Triangle/star-triangle shapes indicate photometric distances; open symbols indicate synthetic W2. The flipbook’s Confirmed only checkbox uses the same standard without changing the overview sample.

Spectral diagnostic panels use the latest active spectrum in each reduction. Mean and median S/N are computed from valid, non-ignored Fλ / uncertainty measurements in the chosen wavelength interval. Stored fit χ² uses the autotype display statistic (robust 10% uncertainty statistic when available); no new fit is run for these panels. CNN probability is P(good) from an autotype result matching the exact object, spectrum and reduction. Missing diagnostics are omitted and counted. Automatic SPIFF selection ranks good/peculiar_ucd/late_M, then contaminated_ucd/good_plus_star, good_candidate, incomplete_but_promising, and other or missing classifications; SPIFFStacker wins ties. Forced lane choices do not fall back to another reduction.

Known UCDs and known UCD candidates are joined to each selected catalog by moca_oid and deduplicated in their union. They and claimed sources bypass each quality group unless its “Also apply filter to known sources” box is checked. Catalog, spectral-type, population, duplicate and photometry selections still apply. Known means a public-adopted nonphotometric M0+ spectral type, including confirmed duplicate links. Use “Recovered known” for public L0+ types. This is a spectral definition, not a mass confirmation. The unpublished population can include unconfirmed candidates.

BYW claims combine the Schneider BYW claimlist with BYW discoverer names containing yadu, tom, bickle, sarah, azul or eastonh, following the reviewer's claim rules. Yadu and Azul memberships remain separate controls. SMDET checks registered designations.

Absolute magnitudes use M = m − 5 log₁₀(d / pc) + 5. Positive adopted parallaxes take priority, followed by adopted nonphotometric distances. Photometric distances are optional and marked separately. They can make a type–magnitude diagram circular. Synthetic W2 comes from stored SPHEREx-linked photometry (or the explicit spherex_w2_synth method), with its measurement and spectrum IDs in CSV. No missing synthetic measurement or distance is inferred.

The Gaia M_G and measured M_W2 diagrams labeled Trigonometric distances use the adopted nonphotometric distance only when it is linked to a parallax; otherwise they invert a positive adopted parallax. Photometric and other nonphotometric distances are excluded. Gaia G uses active, nonsynthetic stored Gaia photometry (adopted simple band first, then adopted measurements, then DR3 / DR2 / DR1), falling back to the stored Gaia DR3 catalog magnitude. Missing magnitudes are counted and omitted. No extinction correction is applied by these diagrams.

Blank numeric cuts keep missing values. An enabled cut requires a measured value. Cutout cuts apply to reviewed cutouts only; “all” requires every stored review to pass, and “any” requires at least one. Unreviewed images are not implicitly good. Duplicates remove only the potential_duplicated_moca_oid side of confirmed “y” pairs; inactive objects are always excluded.
